# AI Usage Policy

This policy sets out how AI tools — coding assistants, LLMs, generators and the
like — may be used when contributing to Homestead. It covers code,
documentation, translations, issues and pull requests.

## Principles

1. **Using AI is allowed and welcome.** AI assistants are legitimate development
   tools, like a linter, a debugger, or API documentation. Using them to write,
   review, translate or refactor is fine.

2. **The contributor is solely responsible.** Whoever opens the issue or the
   pull request takes full responsibility for what they submit — correctness,
   quality, licensing and fit with the project's rules — whether AI was involved
   or not. The tool answers for nothing; the person operating it answers for
   everything. Which means: **review and understand what you submit.** Sending
   code you do not understand is not acceptable, AI-generated or otherwise.

3. **No AI co-authorship.** Authorship is human. Do not credit an AI as
   co-author in commits, pull requests, documentation or any other artefact — no
   `Co-Authored-By:` pointing at an assistant, no "generated by" signatures. The
   reason is plain: a person is using the tool and submitting the result, not an
   AI acting and submitting on its own. Credit and responsibility belong to the
   contributor.

4. **No prejudice against AI-assisted work.** A contribution is not rejected,
   dismissed or treated with suspicion *merely because AI helped produce it*.
   Review judges the contribution on its merits — correctness, clarity,
   adherence to the project's rules — never on the tool behind it. Disclosing AI
   use is **optional**: nobody has to say, and anyone who wants to mention it
   ("reviewed by me, drafted with X") is welcome to. Neither forbidden nor
   required.

5. **Review may use AI too.** Maintainers and reviewers may lean on AI when
   reviewing, under the same rule as point 2: the tool assists, the judgement
   and the responsibility stay human. An AI does not approve or reject a pull
   request on its own.

## What to watch for in AI output

Point 2 has concrete consequences here, because assistants fail in specific
ways. Before submitting, check these in particular:

- **Invented APIs.** This is the failure mode that matters most for this
  project. The Bedrock Script API changes between versions and is poorly covered
  in training data, so assistants routinely produce methods, events and
  components that do not exist, or that existed in 1.21 and were removed by
  1.26. Confirm every API call against the real
  [`@minecraft/server`](https://learn.microsoft.com/minecraft/creator/scriptapi/)
  documentation for the version in `manifest.json`. `python tools/check.py`
  catches broken imports and missing config keys, but it cannot tell you whether
  `player.someMethod()` exists.

- **Behaviour that cannot be verified statically.** Nothing in this repository
  executes JavaScript; the game is the only test. A change that looks right and
  was never run is exactly the kind of thing an assistant produces confidently.
  Say in the pull request what you tested in game, or say plainly that you did
  not.

- **Non-existent or malicious dependencies.** Do not add a package because an AI
  suggested it; plausible names may not exist, or may be typosquatting. The
  add-on itself has no dependencies, and the docs toolchain in
  `requirements-docs.txt` is pinned on purpose. Check the official source before
  proposing a change there.

- **Licensing of suggested code.** Generated snippets can reproduce third-party
  code under an incompatible licence without saying so. Anything that comes in
  needs a licence compatible with the [MPL-2.0](LICENSE) and proper attribution.

- **Secrets and personal data.** Do not paste keys, tokens or personal data into
  prompts, and do not accept examples that embed them in code.

## What still applies

This policy loosens nothing. Everything submitted, AI-assisted or not, must:

- Follow the conventions in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) — en-US source,
  player text through translation keys, Latin-1 in displayed strings, comments
  that explain why rather than what.
- Pass `python tools/check.py` and build with `python build.py`.
- Record any API constraint it uncovers in
  [`docs/architecture.md`](docs/architecture.md), so the next person does not
  pay to learn it twice.
- Respect third-party licences, and introduce no secrets, personal data, or
  content you do not have the right to contribute.
